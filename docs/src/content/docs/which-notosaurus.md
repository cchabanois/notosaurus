---
title: "Which Notosaurus?"
description: "The Android app on its own, or the Anki add-on on a computer: what each needs, what it costs, where the lessons are kept."
---

Notosaurus comes in two forms. Both make the same cards, from the same photos, with the same
instructions and review; they differ in where Notosaurus runs and who pays for the AI.

- **The Android app**: everything on the phone. No computer, no AI key: a subscription pays for
  the AI.
- **The Anki add-on**: Notosaurus runs on the computer, inside Anki desktop, and you use it from
  the phone on the same Wi-Fi. Free, with your own key for an AI service.

| | Android app | Anki add-on |
|---|---|---|
| **What you need** | An Android phone, AnkiDroid | A computer with Anki desktop, a phone on the same Wi-Fi (Android or iPhone) |
| **iPhone** | No: Android only | Yes: the page in Safari, the cards reviewed in AnkiMobile |
| **The AI** | Google's Gemini, through the Notosaurus service | The service you choose: Gemini, Claude, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or a model at home |
| **What it costs** | A subscription through Google Play, after a free trial | Free; the AI service bills you directly, from a fraction of a cent to a few cents per lesson |
| **The lessons** | Kept on the phone | Kept on the computer |
| **The cards** | Straight into AnkiDroid | Into Anki desktop, then to the phones by AnkiWeb; or as a `.apkg` file |
| **Several children** | A card per child | A card per child, and an Anki profile per child if you like |

Both at once? The app can also work **with your computer**: it then shows the add-on's page, and
the lessons, the AI and the cards stay on the computer, for free.
