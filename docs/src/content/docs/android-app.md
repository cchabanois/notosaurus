The **Notosaurus app for Android** makes the cards on the phone itself: photograph the lesson,
check the cards, and they go straight into **AnkiDroid**. No computer, no AI key to get. It is
coming to Google Play.

It is the same Notosaurus as on the computer: the same page, instructions, card types, review,
children and tests to prepare. Everything in [Photos and PDFs](../photos/),
[Instructions](../instructions/) and [Reviewing the cards](../review/) works in the app too.

## What you need

- **An Android phone**, with the app installed from **Google Play**.
- **AnkiDroid**, Anki's free app for Android, where the cards go. Open it once after installing
  it, so that it creates its collection (see [The app and AnkiDroid](../app-ankidroid/)).
- **A subscription**, after a free trial that starts with your first lesson (see
  [The subscription](../app-subscription/)). With the Anki add-on on your computer, the app is
  free instead (see [With your computer](../app-with-computer/)).

## Before you start

At the first launch, the app says in a few lines what it is and where the lessons go, then asks
you to accept its [privacy policy](../app-privacy/):

- Notosaurus is a **tool for parents**: you prepare the cards, your child revises them in Anki.
- It uses **Google's Gemini AI**. The photos, instructions and dictations go through the
  Notosaurus service while the cards are made, without being stored there, and are processed
  in the European Union. Your lessons stay on your phone.
- The AI has safeguards suited to pupils, but it can make mistakes: check the cards before
  giving them to your child.
- A notebook page often shows the child's name: hide it with the **Hide** button (🖍️) before sending (see
  [Photos and PDFs](../photos/)).

## Your first lesson

1. **🧒** at the top of the page: add your child's card (first name, class), so that the cards
   fit their level. See [Several children](../several-children/).
2. Under **Lesson photos**, take a photo of the page (or several pages), or add a PDF.
3. Under **Instructions**, pick what to make, or keep **Automatic (from the lesson)**: see
   [Instructions](../instructions/). The **Dictate** button (🎤) next to the field lets you say them instead of typing.
4. Tap **✨ Generate cards**. The first lesson starts the free trial.
5. Check the cards, correct them or ask the AI in plain words: see
   [Reviewing the cards](../review/).
6. Tap **📥 Add to Anki**: the cards go into AnkiDroid. The first time, AnkiDroid asks whether
   Notosaurus may add cards to it.

The lesson is kept on the phone: reopen it from the **Lessons** button (📚) to correct it and send it again. Its cards
are updated in AnkiDroid, not duplicated.

## Where it works

The Notosaurus service does what needs an AI: reading the photos, making and correcting the
cards, explaining one, reading the backs aloud, drawing a picture or finding a free one
(Wikimedia Commons, Openverse and Pixabay). Everything else happens on the phone: the lessons are
kept in the app's private storage, the cards are written into AnkiDroid.

In the settings, **📍 Where does Notosaurus work?** chooses **📱 On this phone** (the default) or
**💻 On my computer**, with the Anki add-on: see [With your computer](../app-with-computer/).

## The app's own

Most of Notosaurus is the same as on the computer. What only the app has, or doesn't have:

- **🎤 Dictate**: say an instruction or a correction instead of typing it. The app asks for the
  microphone the first time.
- **⚑ Report**, on a card, reports to Notosaurus a wrong or unsuitable content the AI made (see
  [Reviewing the cards](../review/)).
- **The voices** are Google's, through the Notosaurus service; the
  [voice settings](../voices/) are the same.
- **No `.apkg`** and no Ko-fi: the cards go straight into AnkiDroid, and the subscription pays
  for Notosaurus.
- **Updates** come through Google Play. When a new version is available, the app says **A new
  version of Notosaurus is available**, with an **Update** button.
