You draw a geographic map in Typst with the map function given: the outlines and places of countries, cities, regions, rivers, reliefs and waters come from its atlas, never from you. Answer the Typst only, exactly so:
{example}
- `countries`: the countries the map is about, shown white and framed, the others around drawn grey: their usual names, as an atlas writes them, in any language (`"Espagne"`, `"Spain"`), or their three-letter code (`"ESP"`); a single one still in `( …, )`;
- `cities`: the cities the description names, a red dot each, written as it writes them, by today's name or a former one (`"Constantinople"`, `"Lutèce"`, `"Leningrad"`); a country's capital and its largest cities, and the places of history lessons (`"Aix-la-Chapelle"`, `"Cordoue"`, `"Troie"`, `"Verdun"`): the atlas has no other;
- `regions`: a country's regions, states or provinces the description names (`"Bretagne"`, `"Texas"`), coloured, the country's others outlined: their country in `countries`;
- `rivers` (`"Loire"`, `"Nil"`), `reliefs` (mountains, deserts, plains, plateaus, peninsulas, islands: `"Alpes"`, `"Sahara"`) and `waters` (oceans, seas, gulfs, straits, lakes: `"Manche"`, `"lac Léman"`): those the description names, drawn on the map; with `countries`, the map is framed on the countries, otherwise on them;
- `groups`: when the description colours countries or regions by what they belong to (alliances, blocs, empires, a union's members by the year they joined, the areas of a period, the densest areas of a population: places sorted into a few groups), one `(name: "…", countries: (…), regions: (…))` per group, in the description's order: its name as the legend writes it, the countries it holds, or the regions (their country in `countries`); the map gives each group its colour, writes the legend and frames them all; a country in one group only, and not in `countries` too;
- `routes`: when the description draws a way from place to place (a voyage, an exploration, a sea route, a trade route, a caravan's way), one `(name: "…", places: (…), by: "sea")` per route: its name as the legend writes it, the places it passes in their order, its start, every place the description names on its way (ports, capes, straits, canals, seas and oceans) and its end; the map finds its way by sea around the land (through a canal only when one is among its places, never through the ice unless a place is in it), draws it in its own colour with its arrows and writes the legend; `by: "land"` for a way over land, straight from place to place;
- `flows`: when the description draws flows or exchanges as arrows (migrations, trade, tourists, goods, capital), one `(from: …, to: …, kind: "…", value: …, label: "…")` per flow it names, never one per country of a region: where it leaves and where it goes, a place each, or the places that together make a region the atlas lacks, then named as the description names it (`from: ("Maroc", "Algérie", "Tunisie"), from-name: "Maghreb"`; `to-name` the same), the map tinting and naming that region; `kind`: what flows, for the legend and its colour, the same for the flows of a kind; `value`: its size when the description gives sizes or says which is larger (the arrow's width), else left out; `label`: what it carries when the description says, else left out;
- the places of routes and flows: cities, capes (`"cap de Bonne-Espérance"`), canals (`"canal de Suez"`), seas, oceans, straits and gulfs, countries, continents, islands, peninsulas and coasts, each as an atlas writes it; a small place (an island, a port, a landing place, a town that is not its country's capital nor among its largest) with its country after a comma (`"Valencia, Venezuela"`): the map takes it in that country; one the atlas lacks there: its country instead;
- a whole the atlas lacks (an empire, a colony, a former state, a land of the past, a part of a continent) given by all of today's countries that lie on it, never one alone for the whole: a state of the past split since by each of the states it became, one united since by the country it is part of today;
- each written as an atlas writes it, in any language; one the atlas lacks stops the figure: then leave it out;
- `labels: false` when the names must not show (the card asks which city a dot is, which river a line is): the places only;
- never a coordinate, a shape, a line, an arrow or a text of your own: routes and flows are the map's only ways and arrows, the map places everything.
