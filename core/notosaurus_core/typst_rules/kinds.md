"geometry": points, segments, lines, circles, polygons, angles, their marks (a theorem, a construction), a number line with its points (a logarithmic scale too: values over several powers of ten), a fraction shown on a shape cut into equal parts (a disc, a bar, a rectangle, some parts coloured); "graph": the graph of one or more functions given by their formula or their properties (a sinusoid of a given amplitude and period) in a frame, or points in a coordinate frame of the plane (reading or placing their coordinates), never a frame of space nor a box plot; "sets": a Venn diagram of two or three sets, or groups inside one another with their members (ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℝ and examples of each, a classification's nested groups), or coloured lights or filters mixed (additive or subtractive synthesis); "diagram": boxes side by side joined by arrows (a flowchart, a cycle, a chain, a classification), or a graph of graph theory (vertices joined by edges, weighted or directed, a probabilistic graph), never shapes inside one another, never a phylogenetic tree, never a Scratch script; "circuit": an electric circuit; "chemistry": a molecule's formula written with its atoms' symbols and bonds (developed, semi-developed, skeletal, a ring), never a model of balls; "timeline": dates on a line, or moments in their order without dates (a verb's tenses: past, present, future); "map": a geographic map of countries, their cities, regions, rivers, reliefs and seas (where a place is), only the places an atlas names: a map of what the lesson places itself (its own zones, lines or points: a boundary, a route, a front, sites of an event) is no map; "clock": a clock's face showing a time (reading the time); "solid": a solid in perspective (a cube, a cuboid, a prism, a pyramid, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere, a half-ball), a crystal (balls, atoms or ions, on a cubic lattice: a unit cell, simple, body-centred or face-centred, or a block of alternating ions), or a coordinate frame of space (O ; i, j, k) with its points, lines, planes and vectors given by coordinates; "chart": a statistics chart from counts (bars, a pie chart, a half pie chart; never a fraction shown on a shape), box plots (boîtes à moustaches: a minimum, quartiles, a median, a maximum, one box or several on one axis), or measures joined by a line or a curve (a heart rate, a concentration over time): measures given value by value are a chart, even when the curve through them is smooth, never a function fitted to them; "sequence": sequences of letters aligned to be compared (DNA, RNA, proteins: two alleles, a mutation, a sequence and its transcript), never boxes joined by arrows; "mechanics": a body and the vectors at it (forces, a velocity, an acceleration, a Frenet frame), on a circle (an orbit), on the ground, on a slope, hanging from a string (a pendulum, a dynamometer), in a liquid (Archimedes' thrust), two bodies acting on each other (gravitation, a magnet and a nail) or alone, or a chronophotography (a body's positions at equal times: thrown, falling, along a line, on a circle, its velocity vectors), never a trajectory's curve without its positions nor an electric field between plates; "phylogeny": a phylogenetic tree (species, the innovations they share on its branches, their kinship); "table": a table of values in rows and columns, numbers or words in its cells (a two-way table and its totals, a proportionality table and its arrows × 3, a conversion table of units, a frequency table, a table of measures, a cross of genotypes), never a table of signs or of variations of a function; "scratch": a Scratch script, its blocks fitted into one another (block programming: a loop, a condition, a block of its own), never a flowchart of an algorithm (a diagram); "other": anything else, never a box plot (a table of signs or of variations, a labelled drawing, zones nested or overlapping that are no groups of members (an organ's parts), a molecular model of coloured balls, never a crystal…)
