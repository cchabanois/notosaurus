"geometry": points, segments, lines, circles, polygons, angles, their marks (a theorem, a construction), a number line with its points; "graph": the graph of one or more functions in a frame, or points in a coordinate frame (reading or placing their coordinates); "sets": a Venn diagram of two or three sets; "diagram": boxes side by side joined by arrows (a flowchart, a cycle, a chain, a classification), never shapes inside one another; "circuit": an electric circuit; "chemistry": a molecule's formula written with its atoms' symbols and bonds (developed, semi-developed, skeletal, a ring), never a model of balls; "timeline": dates on a line; "map": a geographic map of countries, their cities, regions, rivers, reliefs and seas (where a place is), only the places an atlas names: a map of what the lesson places itself (its own zones, lines or points: a boundary, a route, a front, sites of an event) is no map; "clock": a clock's face showing a time (reading the time); "solid": a solid in perspective (a cube, a cuboid, a prism, a pyramid, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere, a half-ball); "chart": a statistics chart from counts (bars, a pie chart, a half pie chart), or measures joined by a line (a heart rate over time); "other": anything else (a table, a labelled drawing, zones nested or overlapping, a molecular model of coloured balls…)
