"geometry": points, segments, lines, circles, polygons, angles, their marks (a theorem, a construction), a number line with its points, a fraction shown on a shape cut into equal parts (a disc, a bar, a rectangle, some parts coloured); "graph": the graph of one or more functions in a frame, or points in a coordinate frame of the plane (reading or placing their coordinates), never a frame of space; "sets": a Venn diagram of two or three sets, or groups inside one another with their members (ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℝ and examples of each, a classification's nested groups), or coloured lights or filters mixed (additive or subtractive synthesis); "diagram": boxes side by side joined by arrows (a flowchart, a cycle, a chain, a classification), or a graph of graph theory (vertices joined by edges, weighted or directed, a probabilistic graph), never shapes inside one another, never a phylogenetic tree; "circuit": an electric circuit; "chemistry": a molecule's formula written with its atoms' symbols and bonds (developed, semi-developed, skeletal, a ring), never a model of balls; "timeline": dates on a line, or moments in their order without dates (a verb's tenses: past, present, future); "map": a geographic map of countries, their cities, regions, rivers, reliefs and seas (where a place is), only the places an atlas names: a map of what the lesson places itself (its own zones, lines or points: a boundary, a route, a front, sites of an event) is no map; "clock": a clock's face showing a time (reading the time); "solid": a solid in perspective (a cube, a cuboid, a prism, a pyramid, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere, a half-ball), or a coordinate frame of space (O ; i, j, k) with its points, lines, planes and vectors given by coordinates; "chart": a statistics chart from counts (bars, a pie chart, a half pie chart; never a fraction shown on a shape), or measures joined by a line (a heart rate over time); "sequence": sequences of letters aligned to be compared (DNA, RNA, proteins: two alleles, a mutation, a sequence and its transcript), never boxes joined by arrows; "phylogeny": a phylogenetic tree (species, the innovations they share on its branches, their kinship); "other": anything else (a table, a labelled drawing, zones nested or overlapping that are no groups of members (an organ's parts), a molecular model of coloured balls…)
