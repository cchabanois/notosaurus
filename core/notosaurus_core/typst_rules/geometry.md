You draw a geometry figure in Typst with ctz-euclide 0.3. Answer the Typst only, exactly so:
{example}
- points: `ctz-def-points(A: (0, 0), B: (4, 0))` (names: letters, digits after them; an image's name with its prime, `"A'"`, written as a key: `ctz-def-points(..("A'": (3, 1)))`, `ctz-draw-labels(..("A'": "above"))`, never another name in its place, `"Ap"` or `"A1"`, nor a text of its own over it); an image by a transformation computed, never placed by eye: `ctz-def-translate("B'", "B", ("A", "A'"))` (the translation taking A to A'), A' itself, when the description gives no vector (only "the translation taking A to A'"), placed by `ctz-def-translation-image("A'", "A", ("A", "B", "C"))` (the figure's vertices in order: its image clear of it, the arrow along no side), never at coordinates of your own, `ctz-def-rotation("A'", "A", "O", 90)` (centre O, 90° anticlockwise, `-90` clockwise), its direction, when the description shows it (an arrowed arc), by `ctz-draw-rotation-arc("A", "O", "A'", label: [90°])` (around O from A to its image, the way it turns, `clockwise: true` for a clockwise one; `label` only when the description writes the angle), never `ctz-draw-arc` with angles or an arrow of your own, `ctz-def-symmetry("A'", "A", "O")` (symmetric about the point O), `ctz-def-reflect("A'", "A", "D1", "D2")` (about the line through D1 and D2); other computed ones: `ctz-def-midpoint("I", "A", "B")`, `ctz-def-ll("O", ("A", "C"), ("B", "D"))` (where lines AC and BD cross), `ctz-def-mediator("M1", "M2", "A", "B")` (two points of AB's perpendicular bisector), `ctz-def-perp("P1", "P2", ("A", "B"), "C")` and `ctz-def-para("P1", "P2", ("A", "B"), "C")` (the perpendicular, the parallel to AB through C), `ctz-def-bisect("B1", "B2", "A", "O", "B")` (the bisector of angle AOB); compute coordinates from the description (a right angle really right, equal sides really equal); where two lines cross (an angle's vertex at a crossing, a line cut by another), the point computed with `ctz-def-ll`, never guessed;
- drawing: `ctz-draw-segment("A", "B")`, `ctz-draw-polygon("A", "B", "C")`, `ctz-draw-circle-through("O", "A")`, `ctz-draw-circle-r("O", 2)`, `ctz-draw-points("O", "I")` (the points to show as dots);
- a whole line: `ctz-draw-line-add("A", "B")` through two of its points near the figure, drawn a little past them for you (as far as the figure allows), never an `add` of your own to carry it farther (`add: (0, 0.3)` only for a half-line from A); the points ctz-def-perp, ctz-def-para and ctz-def-mediator give are near: on each side of the point, at the reference segment's length (the tangent at A to a circle of centre O: `ctz-def-perp("T1", "T2", ("O", "A"), "A")`, then the line through T1 and T2 as they are); ctz-def-bisect's are far: for a bisector, define two points of your own on it, near the figure; the line's name next to it;
- a vector (named with an arrow, $arrow(u)$, $arrow(A B)$, or a force, a speed): `ctz-draw-vector("A", "B", label: $arrow(A B)$, away: "C")` (its arrow from its origin to its end), every vector the description names drawn so, never as a segment;
- marks, never drawn by hand: `ctz-draw-mark-segment("A", "O", mark: 1)` (equal lengths: the same `mark`, 1, 2 or 3 ticks), `ctz-draw-mark-right-angle("A", "I", "B")` (a right angle at the middle point), `ctz-draw-angle("O", "A", "B", radius: 0.6)` (the vertex FIRST: the angle at O between OA and OB, always the smaller one; `fill: luma(225)` shaded, `label: $alpha$`; the same angles marked the same way);
- labels: `ctz-draw-labels(A: "below left", B: "above")` (each its side: above, below, left, right, above left…), only the asked ones, never the points made to draw something; a point the description names otherwise than by a letter (a number, a complex number, $z_1$, $j^2$): its key a letter, its text given, `ctz-draw-labels(B: (pos: "above left", label: $j$))`, the only name written by it (never a letter of your own beside it, nor a `ctz-draw-text` placed near a point); a segment's name or length: `ctz-draw-length("A", "B", [3 cm], away: "C")` (beside the segment, on the side away from the figure's point `away`: never on its line), `$r$` a name; every length the description gives written on the very segment it names, as it gives it (AB = 5 cm on [AB], never turned into another segment's length, MB = 3 cm, computed from it); a segment holding another one whose length is written ([AB] around [AM]): `level: 2` (a dimension line beside it); the one it asks for marked as it says (`[?]`); a point on a side at a given length (M on [AB] with AM = 2 cm, AB = 5 cm): computed, `ctz-def-linear("M", "A", "B", 2/5)` (at AM/AB of the way from A to B), never placed by eye; a line's name, as the description writes it: `ctz-draw-text(2.2, 2, $(d)$)` (x, y next to the line);
- a number line (graduated), everything on it from its values: `ctz-draw-number-line(-5, 5, points: (A: -3, B: 2))` (its first and last ticks; `step` the graduation, a tick each step, the unit cut in 4: `step: 0.25`; one `scale` for everything, its length (last − first) × scale about 10: from 0 to 2 `scale: 5`, from 0 to 1000 `step: 100, scale: 0.01`, from 2 to 3 `step: 0.1, scale: 10`); `numbers`: its whole numbers written by default, `numbers: false` none, or only those the description writes, as values (`numbers: (2.3, 2.4)`, written with the language's comma) or a value and its text (`numbers: ((1/3, $1/3$), (2/3, $2/3$))`); `points` each point the description places, its letter at its value, a number (`points: (E: 2.7)`, a fraction as a division: `points: (B: 3/4, K: 7/5)`), its letter the only thing written for it (its value is the line's to show, never written as its name); a point the description names by no letter: `(P: (at: 0.75, name: $j$))`; nothing else written; `arrow: true` only when the description says so; a half-line from its first tick the same; every point on it, whatever it stands for, through its `points` (its names, too close, raised for you), never a tick, a number, a point or a name placed by hand (`ctz-def-points`, `ctz-draw-points`, `ctz-draw-text` at coordinates of your own, even computed from its `scale`: the line's coordinates are not its values); values spread over several powers of ten (or a logarithmic scale asked): `ctz-draw-number-line(log: true, points: (M: (at: 2 / 1000, name: [mm]), K: (at: 3e3, name: [km])))`, no first and last (its decades from its values), each value as the description gives it, turned into the line's one unit by an expression (`2 / 1000`), never its logarithm computed by you; its ticks the powers of ten, written 10ⁿ by it (`numbers: (5, 500)`: only the values the description writes, each on a tick of its own); an interval on it (a set of solutions): the line, then `ctz-draw-interval(-1, 3, closed: (true, false), first: -5, last: 5)` (its two bounds as values, `none` for an infinite end, the line's `first`, `last` and `scale` given again, the same; on a logarithmic line `ctz-draw-interval(5, 500, log: true)`, its bounds alone), never a bracket drawn by hand; a stretch the description names (a range, a zone of a scale, from a value to another or beyond one): an interval, `closed: none` (no bracket), its name its `label: [...]` (written under it, zones side by side coloured apart, all by the line), never a point nor a text for a stretch;
- a fraction shown on a shape (a disc, a bar or a rectangle cut into equal parts, some of them coloured), alone, no canvas: `#fraction-shape(kind: "disc", parts: 4, coloured: 3)` (`"bar"`, `"rect"` with `rows: 2`; `caption: $3/4$` only when the description writes it), several side by side (a fraction over 1 on several shapes, two fractions compared): `#fraction-shapes((kind: "disc", parts: 3, coloured: 3), (kind: "disc", parts: 3, coloured: 2))`, every shape the same size, two compared of the same kind (a rectangle beside a rectangle, never a bar: the same whole); its parts and coloured parts as the description counts them, never a part coloured by hand;
- a coordinate frame (points, a line, a circle given by their coordinates or an equation): `ctz-draw-grid(xmin: -2, xmax: 6, ymin: -3, ymax: 5, stroke: luma(220) + 0.4pt)` then `ctz-draw-axes(xmin: -2, xmax: 6.5, ymin: -3, ymax: 5.5)`, named arguments only (never `ctz-draw-axes(-2, 6, -3, 5)`), the grid a unit beyond the figure on each side, the axes half a unit beyond the grid (their arrows clear of the last numbers); the axes graduate and number themselves; each point then at its coordinates;
- the trigonometric circle: `ctz-draw-unit-circle(angles: ((0, $0$), (60, $pi/3$)), project: (60, $1/2$, $sqrt(3)/2$))` (each point's angle in degrees and its name as the description writes it; `project`: the angle whose cosine and sine are shown, then their values), its axes, circle, points and projections all drawn by it, nothing of them by hand; points on the circle of radius 1 joined (a regular polygon inscribed, the n-th roots of unity): `join: true`, the angles in their order around it, never a frame and a circle of your own;
- angles in degrees as numbers where a function takes one (`ctz-def-rotation("B", "A", "O", 60)`).
