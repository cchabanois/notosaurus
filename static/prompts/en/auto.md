Look at the lesson and choose the most useful cards to review it:
- vocabulary of a language: one card per word or phrase, front in my language, back in the language being learned;
- a lesson (history, science, geography…): short questions / answers, or fill in the blanks for the sentences to remember;
- formulas: one card per formula (front: what it calculates, back: the formula);
- a notion a figure makes clear (geometry, or any figure of the lesson): cards with an exact figure when it helps;
- a diagram with labels: a diagram to complete, one card per label;
- a dictation to prepare (a text or a list of words to learn to spell): dictation cards only, for the whole lesson; for each word, front: a short sentence using it, the word replaced by "…", back: the word alone; for a text, one card per sentence, front: "Sentence 1", "Sentence 2"…, back: the exact sentence, with its punctuation;
- a table to know by heart (declension, conjugation, irregular verbs…): one card per cell, except the one the model word comes from (its base form) (front: the model word and the cell's headings, back: the cell, info: the ending), or one per row when each row is a different word (front: the first item and, in brackets, the other columns' headings, back: those columns separated by commas); typed answer;
- dates and landmarks (a timeline, dates to remember): one card per landmark, front: the event, back: its date, info: the century; with the reverse card;
- properties and theorems (a “Property”, “Theorem” box): a card name → full statement (“If …, then …”), a card hypotheses → conclusion, the converse on its own when the lesson gives it; info: what it is for;
- notions shown with examples (figures of speech, word classes or grammatical functions…): one card per example of the lesson, front: a short question saying what to recognise, then the example, back: the notion, info: its definition; never a made-up example;
- a text to learn by heart (a poem, a recitation, a speech; not a dictation): one card per line from the second, front: the title, the line number and the two lines before, back: the line, a stanza's start said on the front; one card title → first line; the exact text; read aloud;
Mix them if the page holds several of these, but a dictation to prepare: then it alone.
Say in one sentence what you chose.
